Collaboration

How CTSA hubs work together. Each blue dot is a hub, and a line joins two hubs that publish together. By default a line's strength is relative: how many more papers the pair shares than you'd expect from how much each hub collaborates overall (×1 = as expected). That shows who prefers whom, rather than just which hubs are biggest. Click a hub to see its strongest partners; switch on partner institutions (orange) or other non-hub institutions (green) to see who else a hub publishes with. Click a line, hub or institution to list its papers in PubMed. "More filters" narrows everything to a theme, a type of funding, one grant or PI, lead authors, or highly cited papers. Zoom with the + and − buttons, a pinch, or Ctrl/⌘ + scroll.

How this is built
  • Hubs: the 67 CTSA hubs (any UL1, or a hub UM1; the FY2026 New Approach Methods UM1 centers are not hubs). Dartmouth's two grantees are one hub.
  • Co-authorship: a hub is on a paper when an author's PubMed affiliation names the hub's own institution (core), or, in the partner network, also one of its affiliated hospitals or institutes. Names come from a curated list.
  • Co-funding: a hub is on a paper when the paper cites that hub's CTSA program award (hub award, KL2/TL1, K12/T32) in NIH RePORTER.
  • Relative link strength (association strength): a pair's shared papers divided by the number expected if each hub spread its collaboration across the others in proportion to their size. ×1 is as expected; ×3 is three times as many shared papers as expected. Pairs need at least 10 shared papers to be ranked this way.
  • Counting: full counting adds 1 for each paper two hubs share. Fractional counting gives each pair on a paper with k hubs 1/(k−1), so a 40-hub consortium paper weighs as much in total as a two-hub paper.
  • Before 2014 PubMed recorded only the first author's affiliation, so co-authorship starts in 2014 by default.
  • Which papers: papers linked to any NCATS grant. A hub that acknowledges its grant on more papers (Yale most of all) contributes more papers, and so more links. Hiding a hub with "drop papers only in the data through a hidden hub's grants" removes that effect.
  • Partner institutions (orange) are the affiliated hospitals and institutes in the curated hub name list, such as Massachusetts General Hospital for Harvard. Other non-hub institutions (green) are parsed from the affiliations of authors who match no hub; only US institutions with a located city, and at least 3 papers with hubs since 2014, can be drawn. Dot size is papers with hubs, or with the selected hub.
  • More filters: theme uses the abstract-based themes (Themes & Translation tab; about 56% of papers have one). Funding keeps papers that cite at least one NCATS grant of that type. Grant or PI keeps papers citing that grant, or any grant on which the person is a PI. First or last author counts a hub on a paper only when the hub has the first or last author (co-authorship networks only). Citation impact uses iCite's Relative Citation Ratio, where 1 is the field average. Translational uses iCite's Triangle of Biomedicine.
  • Locations: each hub sits at its city's center point from the US Census Gazetteer; hubs in the same city are spread slightly so each is visible. State outlines: US Census Bureau 2024 cartographic boundary file.
